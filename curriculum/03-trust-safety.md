# Module 3 — Trust & Psychological Safety (Days 13–18)

> "Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind." — Brené Brown, *Dare to Lead*

Competency: **Trust**

---

## Day 13 — Trust Is the Foundation
**Inspired by:** Patrick Lencioni, *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team*.
**Big idea:** Teams fail in a pyramid: **absence of trust** → fear of conflict → lack of commitment → avoidance of accountability → inattention to results. Fix the bottom first.
**Story hook:** In Lencioni's fable, new CEO Kathryn inherits a brilliant executive team that can't function. Her first move isn't strategy; it's getting them to share personal histories and admit weaknesses.
**Teach:**
- Lencioni's trust = **vulnerability-based trust**: being able to say "I was wrong", "I need help", "You're better at this than I am."
- Without trust, people hold back in debates, then don't commit, then don't hold each other accountable.
- Families have the same pyramid.
**Practice — Work:** Diagnose your team: which level of the pyramid is weakest? Evidence?
**Practice — Home:** Can each person in your family say "I messed up" without fear? Who can't, and why?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why does fear of conflict come from lack of trust? (A: without trust, disagreement feels like an attack, so people stay polite and silent.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — say "I need help with…" to someone on your team. Home — tell your partner/kid about a mistake you made today.
**Reflection:** Do people feel safe being vulnerable around me?

## Day 14 — Vulnerability & Courage
**Inspired by:** Brené Brown, *Dare to Lead*; Ed Catmull, *Creativity, Inc.*
**Big idea:** Vulnerability isn't weakness — it's uncertainty, risk and emotional exposure, and it's the birthplace of trust and innovation. And clarity is a form of respect: "Clear is kind."
**Story hook:** At Pixar, early versions of every film are, in Catmull's words, bad ("all our movies suck" at first). The **Braintrust** gives candid notes; directors keep authority to decide. Candor without fear is how *Toy Story* gets made.
**Teach:**
- Armored leadership: perfectionism, cynicism, knowing instead of learning, "power over."
- Daring leadership: curiosity, "I don't know", clear expectations.
- Unclear = unkind: avoiding a hard truth to be "nice" hurts people later.
**Practice — Work:** What's one expectation you've been vague about? Make it clear in one sentence.
**Practice — Home:** What's one thing you've never told your partner/kids that would help them understand you?
**Check yourself:** Q: Give an example of "unclear is unkind" at work.
**Today's challenge:** Have one "clear is kind" conversation you've been postponing (small one is fine).
**Reflection:** What armor do I wear most?

## Day 15 — Psychological Safety
**Inspired by:** Amy Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization*; Google's Project Aristotle; Alan Mulally at Ford.
**Big idea:** Psychological safety is "a belief that one will not be punished or humiliated for speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns or mistakes" (Edmondson). Google found it was the #1 factor in effective teams.
**Story hook:** At Ford in 2006, Mulally ran weekly reviews with color-coded status. Despite billions in losses, every chart was green. When Mark Fields finally showed a red one, people expected him to be fired. Mulally clapped and said something like "That's great visibility." The next week, the charts lit up with honest colors.
**Teach:**
- Edmondson's leader toolkit: **frame the work** (it's uncertain, we need your voice), **invite participation** (ask genuine questions), **respond productively** (thank, not punish).
- The leader's reaction to the first bad news sets the culture.
**Practice — Work:** Someone tells you they made a costly mistake. Write your first two sentences.
**Practice — Home:** Your kid confesses they broke something. Same exercise.
**Check yourself:** Q: Is psychological safety the same as being nice/low standards? (A: no — high safety + high standards = learning zone.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — ask in a meeting "What am I missing?" and thank the first person who disagrees. Home — thank someone for telling you an uncomfortable truth.
**Reflection:** How do I react to bad news — honestly?

## Day 16 — The Speed of Trust
**Inspired by:** Stephen M.R. Covey, *The Speed of Trust*.
**Big idea:** Trust = **character** (integrity, intent) + **competence** (capabilities, results). When trust is high, speed goes up and cost goes down; low trust is a tax on everything.
**Story hook:** Covey Jr. tells of Warren Buffett buying McLane Distribution from Walmart — a company with roughly $23B in revenue, agreed in a two-hour meeting and a handshake and closed in under a month, with almost no formal due diligence. Trust made it fast.
**Teach:**
- Behaviors that build trust: talk straight, demonstrate respect, create transparency, right wrongs, show loyalty, deliver results, keep commitments, extend trust.
- **Keep commitments** is the fastest builder — and the fastest destroyer. Kids track every broken "we'll see" and "later."
**Practice — Work:** Is your team's trust in you more about character or competence? Where's the gap?
**Practice — Home:** List promises you've made to your kids/partner recently. Kept?
**Check yourself:** Q: Can you trust someone's character but not their competence? Example?
**Today's challenge:** Make one small promise at work and one at home — and keep both today, visibly.
**Reflection:** What am I promising too easily?

## Day 17 — Leaders Eat Last
**Inspired by:** Simon Sinek, *Leaders Eat Last*; Bill Campbell.
**Big idea:** People feel safe when leaders sacrifice their own comfort for them — the **Circle of Safety**. In the US Marine Corps, officers eat last.
**Story hook:** Sinek tells of Bob Chapman at Barry-Wehmiller. In the 2008 recession, instead of layoffs, everyone took four weeks of unpaid furlough. People started trading furlough time to cover colleagues who couldn't afford it.
**Teach:**
- Leaders give time and attention, not just money.
- Protecting the team from outside chaos is part of the job.
- At home: parents "eat last" too — but also need to refuel. Sacrifice ≠ self-neglect.
**Practice — Work:** Where could you absorb pressure so your team doesn't have to?
**Practice — Home:** What "small sacrifice" would mean the most to your family this week?
**Check yourself:** Q: What's the danger of a leader who protects themself first? (A: people stop protecting each other; politics replace cooperation.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — take one unpleasant task off someone's plate. Home — give 30 fully phone-free minutes to one person.
**Reflection:** Who feels safe because of me?
