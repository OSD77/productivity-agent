## Day 55 — How Will You Measure Your Life?
**Inspired by:** Clayton Christensen, "How Will You Measure Your Life?" (HBR, 2010) and the book.
**Big idea:** High achievers over-invest in what gives quick, visible returns (work) and under-invest in what pays off slowly (family). Strategy is how you **allocate your time and energy**.
**Story hook:** Christensen, a devout man, decided not to play in the final of a basketball tournament at Oxford because it fell on a Sunday, against his commitment. He concluded: **it's easier to hold your principles 100% of the time than 98% of the time** — the "just this once" marginal-cost trap is how people end up in places they never intended.
**Teach:**
- Your real strategy = where your hours go, not what you say matters.
- Relationships with family and close friends are the deepest source of happiness, but need investment *before* you see you need it.
- Decide your lines in advance.
**Practice — Work:** Where are you tempted by "just this once" at work?
**Practice — Home:** Track: last week's hours, family vs work vs self. Does it match your values?
**Check yourself:** Q: What's the "marginal cost" trap?
**Today's challenge:** Write 3 lines you won't cross (100%, not 98%).
**Reflection:** If I keep allocating time like this for 10 years, where do I end up?

## Day 56 — Getting Out of the Box
**Inspired by:** The Arbinger Institute, *Leadership and Self-Deception*.
**Big idea:** When we betray our own sense of what we should do for someone (**self-betrayal**), we start seeing them as objects — obstacles, vehicles or irrelevancies — and justify ourselves. We're "in the box," and we provoke the very behavior we blame them for.
**Story hook:** Arbinger's classic: a father hears his baby cry at night and feels he should get up so his wife can sleep. He doesn't. Then he starts thinking: "She's lazy… I work hard… she should get up." His self-justification turns his wife into the problem.
**Teach:**
- Signs you're in the box: blaming, inflating others' faults, feeling like a victim, needing to be right.
- You get out by seeing people as people — with needs as real as yours.
- Most "people problems" at work are box problems.
**Practice — Work:** Who at work is "the problem"? Tell the story from their side.
**Practice — Home:** Recall your most recent resentment at home. Was there a self-betrayal before it?
**Check yourself:** Q: How does being in the box provoke the behavior we complain about?
**Today's challenge:** Act on one impulse to help someone today — before your mind talks you out of it.
**Reflection:** Who do I currently see as an object?

## Day 57 — Meaning Under Pressure
**Inspired by:** Viktor Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning*.
**Big idea:** Frankl, a psychiatrist, survived Nazi concentration camps and observed that those with a **why** could bear almost any **how** (quoting Nietzsche). Meaning comes from work/deeds, love, and the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering.
**Story hook:** In the camp, Frankl kept himself alive by imagining his wife, and by picturing himself after the war giving lectures on what he was learning. He did exactly that.
**Teach:**
- Leaders help people find meaning in their work and suffering — not by preaching but by connecting tasks to people who benefit.
- Choose your attitude: the last human freedom.
**Practice — Work:** How could you connect your team's daily work to a real person who benefits?
**Practice — Home:** What gives your family meaning beyond comfort?
**Check yourself:** Q: Frankl's three sources of meaning?
**Today's challenge:** Tell one team member exactly who benefited from their work — with a name or a story.
**Reflection:** What's my why, now — compare to Day 7.

## Day 58 — Growing Other Leaders
**Inspired by:** John Maxwell's Law of Reproduction; Marquet; Collins.
**Big idea:** The ultimate test of a leader isn't followers — it's **other leaders**. At home, the goal of parenting is to raise adults who can lead themselves.
**Story hook:** When Marquet left the Santa Fe, it continued to perform well, and an unusually high number of its officers went on to become submarine commanders. The leadership was in the system, not in the captain.
**Teach:**
- Identify potential, give real responsibility early, coach, then step back.
- Teach your *thinking*, not just your answers.
- At home: give age-appropriate leadership roles to kids (planning a trip, leading a family meeting).
**Practice — Work:** Who's your successor? What would they need to be ready in 12 months?
**Practice — Home:** What leadership role could each child take this month?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why is "succession" a measure of leadership?
**Today's challenge:** Have one development conversation: "Where do you want to be in 2 years, and how can I help?"
**Reflection:** Who am I growing?

## Day 59 — The Five Practices
**Inspired by:** James Kouzes & Barry Posner, *The Leadership Challenge* (decades of research on "personal best" leadership).
**Big idea:** Exemplary leaders do five things: **Model the Way, Inspire a Shared Vision, Challenge the Process, Enable Others to Act, Encourage the Heart**. This synthesizes the whole course.
**Story hook:** Kouzes & Posner asked thousands of people to describe their "personal best" as a leader. Ordinary people — not just CEOs — told the same patterns. Leadership is a learnable set of behaviors.
**Teach:** Map each practice to course modules:
- Model the Way → Modules 1, 10
- Inspire a Shared Vision → Module 2
- Challenge the Process → Modules 7, 9
- Enable Others to Act → Modules 3, 5, 6
- Encourage the Heart → Modules 4, 5
**Practice:** Rate yourself on each practice at work and at home. Where's the biggest gap between the two?
**Today's challenge:** Draft your leadership credo (one page): what you believe, how you lead, what people can expect from you. Bring it tomorrow.
**Reflection:** What story do I want people to tell about me as a leader?
