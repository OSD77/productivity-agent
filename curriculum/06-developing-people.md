# Module 6 — Developing People (Days 31–36)

> "Your title makes you a manager. Your people make you a leader." — attributed to Bill Campbell, *Trillion Dollar Coach*

Competency: **Developing people**

## Day 31 — Drive: What Really Motivates
**Inspired by:** Daniel Pink, *Drive*.
**Big idea:** For anything beyond simple mechanical work, carrots and sticks backfire. People are motivated by **Autonomy, Mastery, Purpose**.
**Story hook:** Pink describes Atlassian's "FedEx Days": engineers get 24 hours to work on anything they want, then "deliver" it to the team the next day. It produced many fixes and new ideas no plan had asked for.
**Teach:**
- Autonomy over task, time, technique, team.
- Mastery: the joy of getting better — needs feedback and "Goldilocks" challenges.
- Purpose: connection to something bigger (Module 2).
- At home: paying kids for grades can turn learning into a transaction.
**Practice — Work:** Pick one team member. Which of A/M/P is most missing for them right now?
**Practice — Home:** How could you give your kid more autonomy over one routine (homework, chores)?
**Check yourself:** Q: When *do* rewards work? (A: routine, algorithmic tasks — and "now that" surprise rewards more than "if-then".)
**Today's challenge:** Give someone autonomy over the *how* of a task you'd normally specify.
**Reflection:** What drives *me* — A, M or P?

## Day 32 — Multipliers vs Diminishers
**Inspired by:** Liz Wiseman, *Multipliers*; Steve Jobs.
**Big idea:** **Multipliers** make everyone smarter and get far more out of people (Wiseman's research: roughly 2x); **Diminishers** drain intelligence. Most diminishers are *accidental* — good intentions, bad effects.
**Story hook:** Jobs: "It doesn't make sense to hire smart people and then tell them what to do; we hire smart people so they can tell us what to do."
**Teach — the 5 disciplines:** Talent Magnet, Liberator (space + high standards), Challenger (stretch questions), Debate Maker, Investor (gives ownership).
**Accidental diminisher traps:** the idea guy, always-on, rescuer, pacesetter, rapid responder, optimist.
**Practice — Work:** Which accidental-diminisher trap fits you? Real example?
**Practice — Home:** "Rescuer" parenting: where do you jump in too fast with your kids?
**Check yourself:** Q: How can an *optimist* be a diminisher? (A: dismissing difficulty makes people feel their struggle isn't seen; it hides risks.)
**Today's challenge:** In one meeting, talk last. At home, let a kid struggle 5 minutes longer before helping.
**Reflection:** Do people get smarter or quieter around me?

## Day 33 — Situational Leadership
**Inspired by:** Hersey & Blanchard; Andy Grove's "task-relevant maturity."
**Big idea:** There's no one best style. Match your style to the person's **competence and commitment for this specific task**: **Directing → Coaching → Supporting → Delegating**.
**Story hook:** Andy Grove (Intel) noted that the right amount of supervision depends on "task-relevant maturity": a veteran on a new task needs structure; a junior person who's mastered a task needs freedom.
**Teach:**
- D1 enthusiastic beginner → **Direct**. D2 disillusioned learner → **Coach**. D3 capable but cautious → **Support**. D4 self-reliant achiever → **Delegate**.
- Over-supervising = micromanaging; under-supervising = abandoning.
- Parenting is the same: a 6-year-old and a 16-year-old need different styles — even the same kid differs per task.
**Practice — Work:** Map 3 people × 1 key task each to D1–D4 and the style you *should* use.
**Practice — Home:** Map your kid on: homework, screen time, cooking.
**Check yourself:** Q: Which stage needs the most emotional support? (A: D2 — skills growing but motivation dipping.)
**Today's challenge:** Change your style with one person today to match their stage.
**Reflection:** Which style do I overuse?

## Day 34 — Delegation & Leverage
**Inspired by:** Andy Grove, *High Output Management*; Ritz-Carlton.
**Big idea:** "A manager's output = the output of their organization + the output of neighboring organizations under their influence." Your job is **leverage**, not doing the work yourself.
**Story hook:** Ritz-Carlton has long allowed employees to spend up to $2,000 per guest to fix a problem, without asking a manager. Delegated authority + clear values = legendary service.
**Teach:**
- Delegate outcomes, not tasks: what + why + constraints + check-in points.
- High-leverage activities: 1:1s, training, decisions that affect many.
- Grove: the 1:1 is the *employee's* meeting — they set the agenda.
- At home: chores with ownership ("you own the laundry") vs tasks ("fold these").
**Practice — Work:** List 5 things you did this week. Which could someone else own? Why haven't you delegated?
**Practice — Home:** What area of the house could a family member fully *own*?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why is training one of the highest-leverage activities?
**Today's challenge:** Delegate one outcome at work and one at home, with the why and a check-in time.
**Reflection:** What am I holding onto because I like doing it?

## Day 35 — Coaching Like Bill Campbell
**Inspired by:** Schmidt, Rosenberg & Eagle, *Trillion Dollar Coach*.
**Big idea:** Campbell, a former football coach, quietly coached Apple, Google and Intuit leaders. His core: **team first, people first, trust, and love** — he hugged people and openly cared.
**Story hook:** Campbell would start meetings with "trip reports" — people shared what they'd done on the weekend. It seemed trivial; it built human connection and trust before hard business.
**Teach:**
- Run a great 1:1: performance on the job, relationships with peers, management/leadership, innovation.
- Lead with "the people are the foundation."
- Coachability: only coach people who are honest, humble, persistent and willing to learn.
**Practice — Work:** Design your next 1:1 with one report using Campbell's structure.
**Practice — Home:** What's the family version of "trip reports"?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why might "trip reports" improve business results?
**Today's challenge:** Start one meeting with a 2-minute human check-in. Do the same at dinner (high/low of the day).
**Reflection:** Do my people know I care about them as people?
