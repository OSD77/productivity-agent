# Module 5 — Feedback & Accountability (Days 25–30)

> "Care personally, challenge directly." — Kim Scott, *Radical Candor*

Competency: **Feedback**

---

## Day 25 — Radical Candor
**Inspired by:** Kim Scott, *Radical Candor* (lessons from Google & Apple).
**Big idea:** Two axes: **Care Personally** and **Challenge Directly**. Both high = Radical Candor. Care without challenge = **Ruinous Empathy** (the most common failure). Challenge without care = **Obnoxious Aggression**. Neither = **Manipulative Insincerity**.
**Story hook:** After Kim Scott's presentation to Google's founders, her boss Sheryl Sandberg pointed out she'd said "um" a lot. Scott brushed it off. Sandberg then said, roughly: "You're one of the smartest people I know, but saying 'um' so much makes you sound stupid." Scott says it was the kindest thing — Sandberg cared enough to be clear.
**Teach:**
- Feedback is about the work/behavior, not the person's identity.
- Praise in public, criticize in private, do it immediately, keep it small and frequent.
- Ask for criticism before giving it.
**Practice — Work:** A well-liked team member produces sloppy work. Which quadrant do you usually fall into? Write the Radical Candor version.
**Practice — Home:** Your partner's habit bothers you, you've been silent for months. Ruinous empathy?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why is ruinous empathy "ruinous"? (A: the person never gets the chance to improve, and gets blindsided later.)
**Today's challenge:** Ask someone at work: "What's one thing I could do or stop doing that would make it easier to work with me?" Then embrace the discomfort — wait 6 seconds of silence.
**Reflection:** Which quadrant am I in at home?

## Day 26 — SBI: Feedback That Lands
**Inspired by:** Center for Creative Leadership's **Situation–Behavior–Impact** model.
**Big idea:** Specific feedback changes behavior; vague feedback ("great job", "be more proactive") doesn't.
**Story hook:** "You're not a team player" makes people defensive. "In Tuesday's planning meeting (S), you interrupted Dana twice (B), and she stopped sharing ideas (I)" opens a conversation.
**Teach:**
- **S**ituation: when/where. **B**ehavior: observable action (what a camera would record). **I**mpact: effect on you/team/results.
- Add **I**ntent: "What was going on for you?" (SBII) — then listen.
- Use SBI for praise too — it's how you get more of the good behavior.
**Practice — Work:** Convert "You need to communicate better" to SBI.
**Practice — Home:** Convert "You're so messy" to SBI — then a praise SBI for something your kid did well.
**Check yourself:** Q: Is "you were rude" a behavior? (A: no, it's a judgment — what did they actually do/say?)
**Today's challenge:** Give one SBI praise at work, one at home, and one SBI correction anywhere.
**Reflection:** Is my feedback specific enough to act on?

## Day 27 — Receiving Feedback
**Inspired by:** Douglas Stone & Sheila Heen, *Thanks for the Feedback*; Ray Dalio.
**Big idea:** The receiver controls whether feedback is learned from. Three **triggers** block us: **truth** (it's wrong), **relationship** (who are *you* to tell me?), **identity** (this shakes who I am).
**Story hook:** Dalio's equation: **Pain + Reflection = Progress**. At Bridgewater, even the founder gets rated by junior employees in real time.
**Teach:**
- Three kinds of feedback: **appreciation**, **coaching**, **evaluation** — mismatches cause frustration ("I wanted thanks, I got coaching").
- Before rejecting it, ask: "What might be right about this?"
- Leaders model receiving feedback — your team copies how you react.
**Practice — Work:** Recall the last feedback that stung. Which trigger was it?
**Practice — Home:** Your kid says "You're always on your phone." Respond in a way that models receiving feedback.
**Check yourself:** Q: Name the 3 triggers and the 3 types.
**Today's challenge:** Ask at home: "What's one thing I do that drives you crazy?" Only allowed responses: "Tell me more" and "Thank you."
**Reflection:** Which trigger fires most for me?

## Day 28 — Accountability Without Micromanaging
**Inspired by:** L. David Marquet, *Turn the Ship Around!*
**Big idea:** Move from **leader–follower** to **leader–leader**: push authority to where the information is. Replace "Request permission to…" with **"I intend to…"**.
**Story hook:** Marquet took command of the USS Santa Fe, then one of the worst-performing submarines in the US Navy. He'd been trained on a different sub; when he gave an order that was impossible on this one, the officer tried to carry it out anyway. Marquet realized a crew that just follows orders is dangerous. He gave control away — and the Santa Fe went from worst to among the best.
**Teach:**
- **Ladder of leadership:** "Tell me what to do" → "I think…" → "I'd like to…" → "I intend to…" → "I've done…".
- Giving control requires **competence** (train) and **clarity** (purpose).
- Accountability = clear expectations + agreed check-ins + follow-through, not hovering.
**Practice — Work:** Someone keeps asking you to approve small decisions. How do you move them up the ladder?
**Practice — Home:** Teen wants a later curfew. How could "I intend to…" work at home?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why does giving control fail without competence and clarity?
**Today's challenge:** When someone asks "What should I do?", answer "What do you think?" / "What do you intend to do?"
**Reflection:** Where am I the bottleneck?

## Day 29 — Praise That Builds Growth
**Inspired by:** Carol Dweck, *Mindset*; Indra Nooyi.
**Big idea:** Praising talent ("you're so smart") builds a fixed mindset and fear of failure. Praising **effort, strategy and progress** builds a growth mindset. The power of **"not yet."**
**Story hook:** As PepsiCo CEO, Indra Nooyi wrote personal letters to the *parents* of her senior executives, thanking them for raising their child. Many parents were moved — and so were the executives. Recognition can be deeply personal.
**Teach:**
- Dweck's research: kids praised for intelligence chose easier tasks afterward; those praised for effort chose harder ones.
- "You haven't mastered it *yet*."
- Recognition should be specific, sincere, timely, and sometimes surprising.
**Practice — Work:** Rewrite "You're a natural at sales" as growth-mindset praise.
**Practice — Home:** Your kid gets a bad grade after studying. What do you say?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why is "you're so talented" risky?
**Today's challenge:** Write a short thank-you note (or message) to someone's family member, mentor or to the person themselves — Nooyi-style.
**Reflection:** Do I praise outcomes or growth?
