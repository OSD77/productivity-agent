# Module 9 — Culture & Change (Days 49–54)

> "The servant-leader is servant first." — Robert K. Greenleaf, *The Servant as Leader* (1970)

Competency: **Culture**

## Day 49 — The Culture Code
**Inspired by:** Daniel Coyle, *The Culture Code*.
**Big idea:** Strong cultures are built through three skills: **Build Safety** (belonging cues), **Share Vulnerability**, **Establish Purpose**. Culture isn't what you say; it's what you repeatedly signal.
**Story hook:** Coyle describes an experiment where kindergartners beat business-school students at building the tallest tower from spaghetti and marshmallows. The MBAs jockeyed for status; the kids just tried, failed and helped each other.
**Teach:**
- Belonging cues: eye contact, listening, "thank you," inclusion, humor.
- Leaders say "I screwed that up" first — the vulnerability loop.
- Purpose is reinforced through simple, repeated catchphrases and stories.
**Practice — Work:** What 3 behaviors define your team's real culture today (not the poster)?
**Practice — Home:** What's your family's unspoken culture? What do your kids think "our family" means?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why did the kids beat the MBAs?
**Today's challenge:** Send 3 belonging cues today: thank someone for something small; include someone who's usually left out.
**Reflection:** What does my behavior teach about "how we do things here"?

## Day 50 — Level 5 Leadership
**Inspired by:** Jim Collins, *Good to Great*.
**Big idea:** The leaders of companies that went from good to great combined **personal humility + professional will**. They use the **window and the mirror**: credit goes out the window to others; when things go wrong, they look in the mirror.
**Story hook:** Darwin Smith, a quiet, unassuming CEO of Kimberly-Clark, made the gutsy decision to sell the company's paper mills and bet the company on consumer products (Kleenex, Huggies). It worked. Collins found celebrity CEOs were often *negatively* correlated with sustained greatness.
**Teach:**
- **First Who, then What:** get the right people on the bus before deciding where to drive.
- Ambition for the cause, not for self.
- Build a team that will succeed without you.
**Practice — Work:** Window and mirror audit: in the last month, when did you take credit? When did you deflect blame?
**Practice — Home:** Where is your family's "right people, right seats" — who's naturally good at what?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why might charisma hurt long-term results?
**Today's challenge:** Publicly credit someone else for a success you contributed to.
**Reflection:** Would my team succeed if I left tomorrow?

## Day 51 — Servant Leadership & Mandela
**Inspired by:** Robert Greenleaf; Nelson Mandela.
**Big idea:** The servant leader's test: "Do those served grow as persons? Do they… become healthier, wiser, freer, more autonomous, more likely themselves to become servants?" (Greenleaf)
**Story hook:** After 27 years in prison, Mandela invited one of his former jailers to his inauguration. In 1995 he wore the Springbok rugby jersey — a symbol of white South Africa — to the World Cup final, turning a symbol of division into one of unity. Mandela said a leader is like a shepherd who sometimes stays behind the flock, letting the nimblest go ahead.
**Teach:**
- Lead from behind when it builds others; lead from the front in danger.
- Forgiveness as a strategic and moral act — not weakness.
- Symbols matter: one gesture can communicate more than a thousand memos.
**Practice — Work:** What symbolic gesture could signal to your team "I'm here to serve you"?
**Practice — Home:** Is there a grudge in your family that, if released, would change the climate?
**Check yourself:** Q: Greenleaf's test — what is it actually measuring?
**Today's challenge:** Ask one person at work and one at home: "How can I serve you better this week?"
**Reflection:** Do the people I lead grow?

## Day 52 — Rituals & Family Culture
**Inspired by:** Bruce Feiler; Covey *7 Habits of Highly Effective Families*; Coyle.
**Big idea:** Families (and teams) are built by **rituals**: repeated, meaningful moments. Research Feiler cites suggests kids who know their family's story — the ups and downs — show more resilience.
**Story hook:** Feiler cites the "Do You Know?" scale (Duke & Fivush, Emory): kids who could answer questions like "Do you know where your grandparents grew up?" scored higher on resilience and self-control. The best is the "oscillating" narrative: "We've had ups and downs, and we always stuck together."
**Teach:**
- Rituals: weekly family dinner, a bedtime question, birthday traditions, team kick-offs, launch celebrations.
- Tell your family's (and company's) story — including the failures.
**Practice — Work:** What ritual could your team own (celebrations, retros, welcome rituals)?
**Practice — Home:** Design one new weekly family ritual.
**Check yourself:** Q: Why the "oscillating" narrative rather than a perfect success story?
**Today's challenge:** Tell your kids (or partner) one story from your family history that includes a hard time.
**Reflection:** What rituals will my kids remember?

## Day 53 — Leading Change
**Inspired by:** John Kotter, *Leading Change*; Chip & Dan Heath, *Switch*.
**Big idea:** Most change efforts fail because of process mistakes (Kotter) and because we speak only to the logical mind (Heath). **Direct the Rider** (logic), **motivate the Elephant** (emotion), **shape the Path** (environment).
**Story hook:** The Heaths tell of Jon Stegner, who piled 424 different gloves his company was buying — at wildly different prices for the same glove — onto the boardroom table. Executives *saw* the waste. Procurement changed immediately. Feel, don't just analyze.
**Teach:**
- Kotter's 8 steps: urgency → coalition → vision → communicate → remove obstacles → short-term wins → build on change → anchor in culture.
- Script critical moves; point to the destination; shrink the change; tweak the environment.
**Practice — Work:** A change your team resists — apply Rider / Elephant / Path.
**Practice — Home:** Reduce screen time at home — design the Path (charging station outside the bedroom?).
**Check yourself:** Q: Why do short-term wins matter in change?
**Today's challenge:** Change one environment cue (desk, meeting, home) that makes the right behavior easier.
**Reflection:** Am I trying to change people with logic alone?
