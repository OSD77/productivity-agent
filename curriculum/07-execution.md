## Day 37 — Important vs Urgent
**Inspired by:** Dwight D. Eisenhower; Covey Habit 3 "Put First Things First"; McKeown, *Essentialism*.
**Big idea:** Eisenhower (quoting a college president): "I have two kinds of problems, the urgent and the important. The urgent are not important, and the important are never urgent." Leaders protect **Quadrant 2**: important, not urgent.
**Story hook:** Covey's "big rocks": put the big rocks (relationships, planning, health) into the jar first, or the sand (email, small stuff) fills it.
**Teach:**
- Quadrants: Q1 crises, **Q2 planning/relationships/prevention**, Q3 interruptions, Q4 waste.
- Essentialism: "less, but better." If it's not a clear yes, it's a no.
- Family time is Q2 — it never screams urgent until it's a crisis.
**Practice — Work:** Map your last 2 workdays into the 4 quadrants. % in Q2?
**Practice — Home:** What Q2 family activity keeps getting pushed by Q3?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why does neglecting Q2 grow Q1? (A: no prevention/planning → more crises.)
**Today's challenge:** Block 60 minutes of Q2 at work this week and one Q2 family event; say one graceful "no."
**Reflection:** What are my big rocks?

## Day 38 — Decision-Making: Doors and Speed
**Inspired by:** Jeff Bezos, Amazon shareholder letters (2015, 2016).
**Big idea:** **Type 1** decisions are one-way doors — irreversible, decide slowly. **Type 2** are two-way doors — reversible, decide fast. Most decisions are Type 2, but big organizations treat them all like Type 1.
**Story hook:** Bezos wrote that most decisions should be made with about 70% of the information you wish you had; waiting for 90% means you're probably slow. And he described "disagree and commit": he once greenlit an Amazon Studios show he doubted — and it went ahead.
**Teach:**
- Classify first: one-way or two-way door?
- Who decides? Make it explicit (decider, consulted, informed).
- **Disagree and commit:** debate fully, then everyone backs the decision.
- At home: let kids own many two-way-door decisions.
**Practice — Work:** List 3 pending decisions. One-way or two-way? Who should make each?
**Practice — Home:** Which family decision are you overthinking that's actually a two-way door?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why is "disagree and commit" better than consensus? (A: speed + full commitment, without fake agreement.)
**Today's challenge:** Make one two-way-door decision today that you've been delaying.
**Reflection:** Am I slow because of risk or fear?

## Day 39 — The 4 Disciplines of Execution
**Inspired by:** McChesney, Covey & Huling, *The 4 Disciplines of Execution*.
**Big idea:** Strategies die in the "whirlwind" of daily work. Execute with: **1) Focus on the Wildly Important (WIG)**, **2) Act on lead measures**, **3) Keep a compelling scoreboard**, **4) Create a cadence of accountability**.
**Story hook:** Lag measures (revenue, weight lost) tell you if you won; **lead measures** (calls made, workouts done) are what you can influence today. Teams that track leads move lags.
**Teach:**
- WIG format: "From X to Y by when."
- Scoreboard: simple enough to see if you're winning in 5 seconds.
- Weekly WIG session: report on commitments, review scoreboard, make new commitments.
- Families can have a WIG (e.g. "dinner together 4 nights/week").
**Practice — Work:** Write one WIG and two lead measures for your team.
**Practice — Home:** Write one family WIG + lead measure + a fridge scoreboard.
**Check yourself:** Q: Lead vs lag for "improve customer satisfaction from 7 to 8.5"?
**Today's challenge:** Share your work WIG with one person; put the family scoreboard on the fridge.
**Reflection:** What's my wildly important goal this quarter?

## Day 40 — Rhythms: Meetings & Family Meetings
**Inspired by:** Patrick Lencioni, *Death by Meeting*; Bruce Feiler, *The Secrets of Happy Families*.
**Big idea:** Meetings aren't the problem; bad meetings are. Different meetings need different purposes. Same at home: a short weekly family meeting changes everything.
**Story hook:** Feiler's family borrowed "agile" from software teams: a weekly family meeting with three questions — **What worked well in our family this week? What didn't? What will we work on next week?** Kids proposed their own rewards and consequences.
**Teach:**
- Lencioni: daily check-in (5 min), weekly tactical (agenda from the lightning round), monthly strategic (one big topic), quarterly off-site.
- Meetings should have conflict (drama), not boredom.
- Every meeting ends with: decisions, owners, what to communicate.
**Practice — Work:** Audit your recurring meetings: kill, shorten or change one.
**Practice — Home:** Plan your first 15-minute family meeting.
**Check yourself:** Q: Why mix strategic and tactical topics in one meeting badly?
**Today's challenge:** Run the family meeting this week; fix one work meeting.
**Reflection:** Which meeting do people dread — and why?

## Day 41 — Principles & Learning From Mistakes
**Inspired by:** Ray Dalio, *Principles*; Peter Drucker, "Managing Oneself."
**Big idea:** Turn experience into **principles** so you decide better next time. Drucker's **feedback analysis**: when you make a key decision, write what you expect; check 9–12 months later.
**Story hook:** In 1982 Dalio publicly and confidently predicted a depression. He was spectacularly wrong, lost nearly everything and had to borrow $4,000 from his father. It taught him to seek out people who disagree with him — and to systematize his decisions.
**Teach:**
- Mistake → reflection → principle → apply.
- Blameless post-mortems at work; "what did we learn?" talks at home.
- "Believability": weigh opinions by track record.
**Practice — Work:** Recall a recent mistake. Write the principle it teaches.
**Practice — Home:** Write one family principle from a recent conflict.
**Check yourself:** Q: What's the purpose of the expectation-vs-outcome log?
**Today's challenge:** Start a decision journal: log one decision with your expected outcome.
**Reflection:** What mistake do I keep repeating?
