## Day 43 — Productive Conflict & Team of Rivals
**Inspired by:** Doris Kearns Goodwin, *Team of Rivals*; Lencioni.
**Big idea:** Great leaders don't avoid conflict — they invite **conflict about ideas** while protecting relationships. Harmony without debate is artificial.
**Story hook:** After winning in 1860, Lincoln appointed the three men he'd beaten for the nomination — Seward, Chase and Bates — to his cabinet. They thought him inferior. He listened to them, absorbed their disagreements, shared credit and took blame. Seward ended up one of his closest allies.
**Teach:**
- Lencioni's conflict continuum: artificial harmony ← → productive conflict ← → destructive attacks.
- "Mining for conflict": ask the quiet dissenter. "Real-time permission": "This is good — keep going."
- Fight about issues, never about identity.
**Practice — Work:** Who would be your "rival in the cabinet" — someone who disagrees and makes you better? How do you bring them closer?
**Practice — Home:** Do your kids see you and your partner disagree respectfully and resolve it?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why might a team with no conflict be a bad sign?
**Today's challenge:** In one meeting, ask "Who sees this differently?" and protect the person who answers.
**Reflection:** Do I surround myself with people who agree with me?

## Day 44 — Getting to Yes
**Inspired by:** Roger Fisher & William Ury, *Getting to Yes* (Harvard Negotiation Project).
**Big idea:** Principled negotiation: **separate the people from the problem**, focus on **interests, not positions**, invent **options for mutual gain**, insist on **objective criteria**. Know your **BATNA**.
**Story hook:** Two sisters fight over an orange and split it in half. One squeezes her half for juice and throws away the peel; the other uses the peel for baking and throws away the fruit. Asking "why do you want it?" would have given each 100%.
**Teach:**
- Position = what they say they want. Interest = why.
- Ask "Why?" and "Why not?" — then brainstorm before deciding.
- BATNA: Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — your walk-away power.
**Practice — Work:** Two team members both want the same project. Uncover interests and design an option for both.
**Practice — Home:** You want a quiet weekend; partner wants to visit family. Interests? Options?
**Check yourself:** Q: Interest behind "I want a 20% raise"? (Several possible — recognition, fairness vs peers, a mortgage…)
**Today's challenge:** In one disagreement, ask "What's important to you about that?" before proposing anything.
**Reflection:** Do I negotiate positions at home?

## Day 45 — Conflict at Home: The Four Horsemen
**Inspired by:** John Gottman, *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*.
**Big idea:** Gottman's research identifies four patterns that predict relationship breakdown — **Criticism, Contempt, Defensiveness, Stonewalling** — and their antidotes. Stable couples keep about **5 positive interactions for every negative one** during conflict.
**Story hook:** In the "Love Lab," Gottman observed couples in conflict and reported he could predict divorce with high accuracy — largely from these patterns, with **contempt** the single strongest predictor.
**Teach (horseman → antidote):**
- Criticism ("You always…") → **gentle start-up** ("I feel… about… I need…").
- Contempt (eye-rolling, sarcasm) → **culture of appreciation**.
- Defensiveness → **take responsibility** for even part of it.
- Stonewalling → **physiological self-soothing** (take a 20-minute break, then return).
- These apply to work teams too.
**Practice — Work:** Which horseman shows up most on your team? What antidote could you model?
**Practice — Home:** Which horseman do *you* bring to arguments? Rewrite your last one with the antidote.
**Check yourself:** Q: Why is contempt so destructive? (A: it communicates disgust/superiority — attacks the person's worth.)
**Today's challenge:** Five specific appreciations at home today. Use one gentle start-up.
**Reflection:** What's my ratio at home this week?

## Day 46 — Leading in a Crisis
**Inspired by:** Ernest Shackleton (*Endurance*, 1914–16); Winston Churchill (1940).
**Big idea:** In crisis, people look to the leader's face. Calm, visible optimism, clear priorities, and caring for each person decide outcomes.
**Story hook:** Shackleton's ship *Endurance* was crushed by Antarctic ice. Stranded for well over a year, he kept morale up with routines, gave his own mittens to a crewman, kept troublemakers close to him, and sailed 800 miles in a lifeboat to get help. Every man of the *Endurance* crew survived.
**Teach:**
- Keep routines and small rituals; they create normality.
- Show optimism *and* realism; never show panic publicly — but be honest.
- Keep dissenters close; one negative person can sink morale.
- Focus on the next step, not the whole mountain.
**Practice — Work:** A major client leaves and the team is scared. What do you do in the first 24 hours?
**Practice — Home:** A family health or job scare. How do you lead the household through it?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why did Shackleton keep the most difficult men in his own tent?
**Today's challenge:** Write your personal "crisis protocol": 3 things you'll do first when things go wrong.
**Reflection:** What do people see on my face under pressure?

## Day 47 — The Obstacle Is the Way
**Inspired by:** Ryan Holiday, *The Obstacle Is the Way*; Benjamin Zander, *The Art of Possibility*.
**Big idea:** Resilience is a discipline of **Perception** (see clearly, without panic), **Action** (do the next right thing), and **Will** (accept what you can't change).
**Story hook:** Zander, a conductor, tells of his "Rule #6": "Don't take yourself so damn seriously." He also teaches musicians to say **"How fascinating!"** after a mistake instead of cursing — turning failure into curiosity.
**Teach:**
- Reframe: "What's the opportunity in this?"
- Separate the event from your story about it.
- Resilience is contagious from leaders and parents.
**Practice — Work:** Take a current obstacle and run it through Perception / Action / Will.
**Practice — Home:** How would your kids describe how you handle setbacks?
**Check yourself:** Q: What's the difference between reframing and denial?
**Today's challenge:** Say "How fascinating!" (out loud) after one mistake today — and teach it to your family.
**Reflection:** What obstacle am I resisting that could become my way?
