# Module 4 — Communication & Listening (Days 19–24)

> "Seek first to understand, then to be understood." — Stephen Covey, Habit 5

Competency: **Communication**

---

## Day 19 — Empathic Listening
**Inspired by:** Covey Habit 5; Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*.
**Big idea:** Most people listen with the intent to reply. Leaders listen with the intent to **understand** — and people don't care how much you know until they feel heard.
**Story hook:** Voss, the FBI's former lead international kidnapping negotiator, says the goal isn't to argue but to get the other side to say **"That's right"** — proof they feel understood.
**Teach:**
- **Mirroring:** repeat the last 1–3 words as a question. "…a disaster?"
- **Labeling:** "It seems like…", "It sounds like you're worried that…"
- **Summarize** until you get "That's right" (not "You're right" — that means "go away").
- Covey's levels: ignoring, pretending, selective, attentive, **empathic**.
**Practice — Work:** A report says "This new process is pointless." Respond with a label and a mirror.
**Practice — Home:** Your kid says "School is stupid." First three responses?
**Check yourself:** Q: Difference between "That's right" and "You're right"?
**Today's challenge:** In one conversation at work and one at home, don't give advice until you've summarized their view and heard "That's right/exactly."
**Reflection:** Who in my life doesn't feel heard by me?

## Day 20 — How to Win Friends and Influence People
**Inspired by:** Dale Carnegie (1936) — still one of the best-selling leadership books ever.
**Big idea:** People are creatures of emotion, not logic. Influence comes from **genuine interest, appreciation and respect for their ego**.
**Story hook:** Carnegie tells how Charles Schwab, whom Andrew Carnegie paid an enormous salary, said his greatest asset was "my ability to arouse enthusiasm among people… by appreciation and encouragement."
**Teach (selected principles):**
- Don't criticize, condemn or complain.
- Give honest, sincere appreciation.
- Remember that a person's name is to that person the sweetest sound.
- Talk in terms of the other person's interests.
- If you're wrong, admit it quickly and emphatically.
- Let the other person save face.
**Practice — Work:** You need help from another team that owes you nothing. How do you ask, Carnegie-style?
**Practice — Home:** Use "the other person's interests" to get your kid to do homework.
**Check yourself:** Q: Flattery vs appreciation? (A: appreciation is sincere and specific; flattery is insincere and self-serving.)
**Today's challenge:** Give 3 specific, sincere appreciations today — at least 1 at home. Zero complaints for 24h.
**Reflection:** How often do I criticize vs appreciate?

## Day 21 — Crucial Conversations
**Inspired by:** Patterson, Grenny, McMillan & Switzler, *Crucial Conversations*.
**Big idea:** When stakes are high, opinions vary and emotions run strong, we tend toward **silence or violence**. Skilled people keep dialogue going by making it **safe**.
**Story hook:** Most of us have had the meeting where everyone nods, then complains in the hallway. That's silence. The opposite — sarcasm, labeling, raised voices — is violence. Both kill the "pool of shared meaning."
**Teach:**
- **Start with heart:** What do I really want for me, for them, for the relationship?
- **Make it safe:** mutual purpose + mutual respect. Use contrasting: "I don't want you to think X. I do want Y."
- **STATE:** Share facts → Tell your story → Ask for their path → Talk tentatively → Encourage testing.
**Practice — Work:** A peer keeps missing commitments that affect your team. Script the opening with STATE.
**Practice — Home:** Your partner and you disagree about money/screen time. Use "start with heart" + a contrasting statement.
**Check yourself:** Q: What's the first sign safety is at risk? (A: the other person goes to silence or violence.)
**Today's challenge:** Use one contrasting statement in a real conversation.
**Reflection:** Do I default to silence or violence?

## Day 22 — Nonviolent Communication at Home
**Inspired by:** Marshall Rosenberg, *Nonviolent Communication*.
**Big idea:** Behind every complaint is an unmet need. Say **Observation → Feeling → Need → Request** instead of judgment.
**Story hook:** Rosenberg describes speaking in a Palestinian refugee camp where a man shouted "Murderer!" because he was American. Instead of defending, Rosenberg guessed at his feelings and needs. After time, the man invited him home for dinner.
**Teach:**
- Observation without evaluation: "You came home at 10" not "You're always late."
- Feelings, not thoughts disguised as feelings ("I feel ignored" is a judgment; "I feel lonely" is a feeling).
- Requests are doable and positive: "Would you be willing to text me if it's after 8?"
**Practice — Work:** Rewrite: "You never update the ticket. It's unprofessional."
**Practice — Home:** Rewrite: "You never help with the kids."
**Check yourself:** Q: Why "would you be willing" and not a demand? (A: a demand invites submission or rebellion; a request invites cooperation.)
**Today's challenge:** Use one full O-F-N-R sentence at home.
**Reflection:** What need is behind my most frequent complaint?

## Day 23 — The Coaching Habit
**Inspired by:** Michael Bungay Stanier, *The Coaching Habit*.
**Big idea:** **Stay curious a little longer; rush to action and advice a little more slowly.** The "advice monster" makes you the bottleneck.
**Story hook:** Bill Campbell, who coached Steve Jobs, Larry Page and Eric Schmidt, rarely told them what to do — he asked questions until they found the answer.
**Teach — the 7 questions:**
1. What's on your mind?
2. And what else? (the best coaching question in the world)
3. What's the real challenge here for you?
4. What do you want?
5. How can I help?
6. If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?
7. What was most useful for you?
**Practice — Work:** Coach roles: learner coaches the coach (playing a stressed report) using only the 7 questions.
**Practice — Home:** Your kid asks how to solve a fight with a friend. Use 3 questions before any advice.
**Check yourself:** Q: Why "for *you*" in Q3? (A: it makes the challenge personal and actionable.)
**Today's challenge:** In your next 1:1, ask "And what else?" at least 3 times.
**Reflection:** When does my advice monster show up?
