# Module 2 — Purpose & Vision (Days 7–12)

> "People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it." — Simon Sinek

Competency: **Vision & purpose**

---

## Day 7 — Start With Why
**Inspired by:** Simon Sinek, *Start With Why*.
**Big idea:** The **Golden Circle**: most people communicate What → How → Why. Inspiring leaders start with **Why** (belief/purpose), then How, then What.
**Story hook:** The Wright brothers had no funding, no college degrees and no press — Samuel Langley had all three. The Wrights were driven by a belief that flight would change the world; Langley wanted to be famous. The Wrights flew first.
**Teach:**
- "Why" isn't "to make money" — that's a result.
- A team that knows the why can make good decisions without you.
- Kids follow rules better when they know the reason ("Because I said so" is pure Level 1).
**Practice — Work:** Write your team's why in one sentence: "We exist to ___ so that ___."
**Practice — Home:** Pick one house rule. Explain its why as you would to your child/partner.
**Check yourself:** Q: Why does starting with "why" make delegation easier? (A: people can make choices aligned with purpose without asking.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — open one request/assignment with the why. Home — explain the why behind one rule instead of just enforcing it.
**Reflection:** What's *my* why as a leader?

## Day 8 — Painting a Vision
**Inspired by:** John F. Kennedy (moon speech), Martin Luther King Jr. ("I Have a Dream").
**Big idea:** A vision is a vivid picture of a better future that people want to help create. It must be **concrete, emotional, and shared**.
**Story hook:** In 1961 Kennedy committed America to landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely "before this decade is out." A legend says a NASA janitor told JFK, "I'm helping put a man on the moon" (likely apocryphal — but it captures what a clear vision does). MLK didn't say "I have a strategic plan"; he described children holding hands.
**Teach:**
- Vision formula: **where we're going + why it matters + what it will look/feel like + a deadline**.
- Specific beats grand: "Every customer gets an answer in under an hour" > "World-class service."
- Repeat it until you're sick of it — then people are just starting to hear it.
**Practice — Work:** Write a 3-sentence vision for your team 12 months from now. Make it something you could *see*.
**Practice — Home:** What does a great Sunday in your family look like in a year? Describe it like a scene.
**Check yourself:** Q: What makes a vision fail? (A: too vague, not shared/co-created, not repeated, contradicted by the leader's behavior.)
**Today's challenge:** Share your work vision draft with one person and ask "What's missing?" Share the home picture at dinner.
**Reflection:** Do my people know where we're going?

## Day 9 — Your Personal & Family Mission Statement
**Inspired by:** Covey Habit 2 "Begin with the End in Mind"; *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective Families*.
**Big idea:** Leaders write the first creation (the plan) before the second (reality). A personal mission statement and a family mission statement act as a compass.
**Story hook:** Covey's exercise: imagine your own funeral. Four speakers — family, a friend, a colleague, someone from your community. What do you want each to say?
**Teach:**
- A mission names **who you want to be** (character) and **what you want to do** (contribution), grounded in values.
- Family missions work best when co-written, even with young kids ("In our family we…").
- Roles: list your key roles (manager, parent, partner, friend) and one goal per role this week.
**Practice — Work:** Draft your funeral speech from a colleague's perspective. What gap do you see?
**Practice — Home:** Draft 3 "In our family we…" lines.
**Check yourself:** Q: Why "begin with the end in mind" before setting goals? (A: so you climb the right ladder — busy ≠ effective.)
**Today's challenge:** Write a first draft of your personal mission (5 lines max). Ask your family one question: "What do we want our family to be known for?"
**Reflection:** Which role am I neglecting?

## Day 10 — Communicating Like Churchill & Lincoln
**Inspired by:** Winston Churchill's 1940 speeches; Lincoln's Gettysburg Address; storytelling.
**Big idea:** Leaders move people with **honesty about reality + hope + simple words + story**.
**Story hook:** In May 1940 Churchill told Parliament: "I have nothing to offer but blood, toil, tears and sweat." He didn't hide the danger — and that's why people believed his hope. Lincoln's Gettysburg Address was ~270 words; the main speaker before him talked for two hours. Nobody remembers that speech.
**Teach:**
- **Confront the brutal facts, keep faith you'll prevail** (Collins calls this the Stockdale Paradox).
- Short words, short sentences, one message.
- Story structure: situation → struggle → turning point → lesson.
**Practice — Work:** Your team must hit a tough target after a bad quarter. Write a 5-sentence message: reality, hope, what we'll do.
**Practice — Home:** The family is moving / tightening the budget. How do you tell the kids honestly *and* hopefully?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why does admitting bad news increase trust in your optimism?
**Today's challenge:** Tell one short story (not a slide) to make a point at work. At home, tell a story from your own childhood that carries a value.
**Reflection:** Do I explain or do I inspire?

## Day 11 — The Infinite Game
**Inspired by:** Simon Sinek, *The Infinite Game*; Satya Nadella, *Hit Refresh*.
**Big idea:** Business, parenting and marriage are **infinite games**: no finish line, no final winner — the goal is to keep playing and get better. Leaders with a finite mindset (winning the quarter, winning the argument) burn trust.
**Story hook:** When Nadella became Microsoft's CEO in 2014 he shifted the culture from "know-it-alls" to "learn-it-alls," focusing on a long-term mission over beating rivals each quarter. Microsoft's culture and value transformed.
**Teach:**
- **Just Cause**, trusting teams, worthy rivals (who reveal your weaknesses), existential flexibility, courage to lead.
- At home: "winning" an argument with your partner is losing the infinite game.
**Practice — Work:** Name one place you're playing finite (a metric, a rivalry). What's the infinite version?
**Practice — Home:** Recall your last argument at home. What would the infinite-game version have been?
**Check yourself:** Q: What's a "worthy rival"? (A: someone whose strengths show you where to improve — not an enemy to beat.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — praise a "rival" for something they do well. Home — let go of being right in one small disagreement.
**Reflection:** What game am I really playing?
