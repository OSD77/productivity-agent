# Module 1 — Lead Yourself First (Days 1–6)

> "Waste no more time arguing about what a good man should be. Be one." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 10.16

Competency: **Self-leadership**

---

## Day 1 — Leadership Is Influence
**Inspired by:** John C. Maxwell, *The 5 Levels of Leadership*; George Washington.
**Big idea:** "Leadership is influence — nothing more, nothing less." A title gives you permission to manage, not to lead. You lead everywhere you have influence: with your boss, your peers, your kids.
**Story hook:** In 1783, Washington could likely have become king. Instead he resigned his commission and went home; later he left the presidency after two terms. King George III reportedly said that if he did that, he'd be "the greatest man in the world." His power came from what he refused to take.
**Teach:**
- Maxwell's 5 Levels: **Position** (people follow because they have to) → **Permission** (because they want to) → **Production** (because of what you've done for the team) → **People Development** (because of what you've done for them) → **Pinnacle** (because of who you are).
- You can be at different levels with different people — Level 4 with one report, Level 1 with your teenager.
- Home has no org chart: there, *all* leadership is levels 2–5.
**Practice — Work:** Map 3 key people (a report, a peer, your boss). What level are you with each? What's the evidence?
**Practice — Home:** If your kid/partner could only follow you because they "have to," what would that look like? Where does that happen today?
**Check yourself:** Q: Why can't Level 1 leadership last? (A: compliance ends when the authority leaves the room; people give the minimum.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — have one conversation where you rely on zero authority, only curiosity. Home — ask one family member "What's one thing I could do that would make your week easier?" and just listen.
**Reflection:** Where do I rely on my position instead of my influence?

## Day 2 — Circle of Influence
**Inspired by:** Stephen Covey, *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* — Habit 1: Be Proactive.
**Big idea:** Proactive people focus on their **Circle of Influence** (what they can affect); reactive people focus on their **Circle of Concern** (what they worry about but can't control) — and their influence shrinks.
**Story hook:** Covey describes the space between stimulus and response — the freedom to choose. (The line is often attributed to Viktor Frankl; Covey popularized it, inspired by Frankl's story, which we'll meet on Day 57.)
**Teach:**
- Reactive language: "There's nothing I can do", "He makes me so mad", "I have to." Proactive: "Let's look at alternatives", "I control my feelings", "I choose to."
- Three kinds of problems: direct control (your habits), indirect control (others' behavior — via influence), no control (accept with grace).
**Practice — Work:** List 5 things frustrating you at work. Sort each into control / influence / no control. For one "influence" item, design the first move.
**Practice — Home:** Your partner leaves the kitchen a mess every night. Reactive vs proactive script?
**Check yourself:** Q: Turn "My boss never gives me clear priorities" into proactive language. (Model: "I'll bring my top 3 priorities to our next 1:1 and ask her to rank them.")
**Today's challenge:** Work + Home — catch yourself using reactive language once in each place and rephrase it out loud.
**Reflection:** What am I spending energy on that's outside my circle?

## Day 3 — Emotional Intelligence
**Inspired by:** Daniel Goleman, "What Makes a Leader?" (HBR, 1998) and *Emotional Intelligence*; Abraham Lincoln's "hot letters."
**Big idea:** IQ and technical skill get you in the door; emotional intelligence — self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill — separates great leaders from the rest.
**Story hook:** After Gettysburg, General Meade let Lee's army escape. Lincoln wrote a furious letter blaming him… and never sent it. It was found after his death, marked "never sent, or signed." Lincoln vented on paper, then chose the response that served the goal.
**Teach:**
- **Amygdala hijack:** under threat, the emotional brain acts before the thinking brain. Name it to tame it: "I'm feeling defensive right now."
- Pause tools: breathe, write the hot letter, delay the reply, ask a question instead of making a statement.
- The 5 components — most leaders are strongest in one and blind in another.
**Practice — Work:** A peer criticizes your project in a big meeting. Walk through: what you feel, what you'd say in the first 10 seconds, what you'd do after.
**Practice — Home:** Your child melts down in a store. What does *your* self-regulation look like before you deal with theirs?
**Check yourself:** Q: Which EI component do you think is your weakest, and what's your evidence?
**Today's challenge:** Work — before one tense reply (email/Slack), wait 10 minutes. Home — name your own feeling out loud once ("I'm frustrated, give me a minute").
**Reflection:** What triggers my hijack most reliably?

## Day 4 — The Inner Citadel (Stoic Self-Command)
**Inspired by:** Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*; Epictetus.
**Big idea:** The most powerful man on earth wrote a private journal reminding himself to be patient, humble and fair. Self-leadership is a daily practice, not a trait.
**Story hook:** Marcus began his day reminding himself he'd meet people who were meddling, ungrateful, arrogant — and that none of them could harm him, because none could make him do wrong (*Meditations* 2.1). He prepared for difficulty instead of being surprised by it.
**Teach:**
- **Dichotomy of control** (Epictetus): some things are up to us (judgments, choices, actions), others aren't.
- **Morning preview / evening review:** What might go wrong today and how do I want to respond? Tonight: where did I act well? Where not?
- Character is what you repeat: "Be one."
**Practice — Work:** Preview tomorrow: which meeting/person is most likely to test you? Pre-decide your response.
**Practice — Home:** Which daily moment (bedtime, morning rush, dinner) tests you most? Pre-decide who you want to be in it.
**Check yourself:** Q: What's the difference between Stoic acceptance and passivity? (A: accept what you can't control so you can act fully on what you can.)
**Today's challenge:** Do a 2-minute morning preview and a 2-minute evening review (write 3 lines).
**Reflection:** Who am I trying to "be" this week — in one word?

## Day 5 — Extreme Ownership
**Inspired by:** Jocko Willink & Leif Babin, *Extreme Ownership*; Eisenhower's D-Day note; JFK after the Bay of Pigs.
**Big idea:** "There are no bad teams, only bad leaders." The leader owns everything in their world — including others' mistakes, unclear orders and missed outcomes.
**Story hook:** Before D-Day, Eisenhower drafted a note in case the landings failed: "…If any blame or fault attaches to the attempt it is mine alone." After the Bay of Pigs, Kennedy publicly took responsibility — and his approval ratings went *up*.
**Teach:**
- Ownership ≠ self-blame. It's: "What could *I* have done to make this go better? What will I do now?"
- Ownership is contagious downward; blame is contagious too.
- Check the orders you gave: were they simple, clear, with the "why"?
**Practice — Work:** A project failed because a team member missed a deadline. Write the ownership version of your debrief.
**Practice — Home:** Your teen keeps forgetting chores. What part of that system do *you* own (clarity, follow-up, example)?
**Check yourself:** Q: Ownership vs. martyrdom — what's the difference? (A: ownership leads to action and improvement; martyrdom is just guilt or show.)
**Today's challenge:** Work — say "That's on me, here's what I'll change" about one real issue. Home — own one recurring friction out loud.
**Reflection:** What am I currently blaming on someone else?

## Day 6 — Module 1 Review & Role-Play
- Mixed quiz (5 Levels, circle of influence, EI components, dichotomy of control, extreme ownership).
- **Role-play:** Coach plays a frustrated team member who blames another team for a delay. Learner must stay regulated, steer from concern → influence, and model ownership.
- **Home role-play:** Coach plays partner complaining "You're always on your phone." Learner practices ownership without defensiveness.
- Re-rate **Self-leadership** 1–10. Pick one Module 1 habit to keep for life (e.g. evening review).
